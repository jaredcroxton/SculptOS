Discovery Qualified calls beat cold calls - prep one sharp question per prospect. Mark Hunter's 2026 prospecting research: cold calling has dropped to a sub-2% success rate (down from 8-10% in the 1990s). The antidote is the qualified call - doing homework first to find a genuine connection or need, then opening with a specific question that demonstrates you know something about their situation. For Accor Plus: the opening question is always about a specific travel friction. "I saw you mentioned Bangkok in a recent post - are you finding the rate volatility there predictable enough to plan around?" This engages because it is specific and relevant. Closing The "Would it be a bad idea to..." close from Chris Voss. Chris Voss's Never Split the Difference technique: replace "Would you like to proceed?" with "Would it be a bad idea to get you set up with a membership before Vivid/your Bangkok trip/your next Bali stay?" The first question prompts a polite rejection. The second prompts an honest reaction - and almost always surfaces the real objection rather than a brush-off. When the prospect says "No, it wouldn't be a bad idea," you have a soft yes. When they name a real concern, you have a real objection to work with. Next action: rewrite your standard close question into Voss's format and use it in your next three calls this week. (Black Swan Group, Negotiation Techniques 2026)
Negotiation LAARC framework: diagnose the objection before responding to it. 2026 B2B objection handling research identifies the LAARC framework (Listen, Acknowledge, Assess, Respond, Confirm) as the most durable approach because the Assess step forces a diagnosis before a rebuttal. Most reps fail at the Assess step - they hear "it's too expensive" and immediately argue on price, when the real objection may be timing, authority, or risk aversion. For membership sales: when a prospect raises any objection, listen fully, acknowledge it ("That's a fair point..."), then ask one diagnostic question before responding ("Is the annual fee the constraint, or is it more about whether the timing works for your travel calendar?"). This changes the conversation from adversarial to collaborative. Next action: add the diagnostic question as a mandatory step in your objection handling process this week. Opener — Compression Event Hook

I work with frequent travellers across APAC who stay at Accor hotels and we make sure they're locked in at member rates before event compression prices them out. Objection Handler — "I already get hotel discounts through my bank card"
"Completely fair - a lot of frequent travellers have card discounts. The difference is that your card gives you a cashback on whatever rate you paid, which is usually not the lowest rate available. ALL Accor+ Explorer gives you a guaranteed 15% off the best available rate on every Accor stay, upfront, with no blackout dates. Plus two-for-one nights, which means one of your APAC hotel stays this year is effectively free. Can I show you the maths on your typical travel spend? It takes about two minutes and it usually changes the conversation."

Why it works: Acknowledges the objection as valid (not a brush-off), draws a sharp factual distinction (cashback on a variable rate vs. guaranteed discount on best rate), uses specificity (15%, two-for-one, no blackout), and closes with a low-friction ask (two minutes to show maths, not a hard sell).
Close — The Vivid/Compression Urgency Close
"Based on what you've told me about your travel to [City] in [Month], this membership pays for itself in the first stay during [Vivid Sydney/Bangkok high season/your next Bali trip]. The rates on those dates are already moving. If we get you set up today, you can book your stay at member rate before the pricing locks in. I can have the link over to you in five minutes - does that work?"

Why it works: References the prospect's own stated travel plans (personalised, not generic), uses factual urgency (real events, real compression), gives a specific time commitment (five minutes), ends with a yes/no question that defaults to action. Replace event names with whatever compression trigger is live in the prospect's key market.
