Market Movers

Australia / NZ

  • Sydney RevPAR is running at $126.81 (up 4.34% year-on-year), with clean weekend ADR hitting $199 versus a $140 weekday rate, signalling strong compression events. Australia remains the one major market globally where RevPAR growth is occupancy-led rather than rate-led, suggesting room to push ADR further. (RevPARGenius, Apr 2026)
  • CBRE estimates 10,500 new international flight routes into Australia will generate 1.9 million additional hotel room nights nationally. Sydney leads with 13 new routes projected to add 542,000 room nights; Melbourne follows with 409,000. (CBRE Australia, 2026)
  • AHICE Asia Pacific 2026 hotel conference takes place May 5-7 in Adelaide, with 1,600 delegates and 250 speakers expected. Accor is delivering a keynote on wellbeing on May 7. Strong signal that the Australian hotel industry is in active deal-making mode. (Hotel Management, Apr 29 2026)
  • Air New Zealand launches a seasonal Christchurch-Rarotonga route beginning May 2026, opening new inbound leisure demand for the Pacific Islands corridor. (Travel & Tour World, 2026)

Japan

  • Japan Q1 2026 visitor spending reached 2.3 trillion yen, up 2.5% year-on-year. Taiwan, South Korea and the US led source market spending. Chinese visitor spending fell 50.4% year-on-year amid ongoing diplomatic friction, redirecting mainland travellers toward Hong Kong and Southeast Asia instead. (Travel & Tour World, May 2026)
  • JTB forecasts 41.4 million international visitors to Japan in 2026, a 2.8% dip from 2025's 42.7 million. The yen has firmed to around 148 against the USD (from a 2025 high of 159), slightly reducing the exchange-rate discount that drove overtourism but still keeping Japan as a strong value destination for many source markets. (Travel Weekly Asia, 2026)

Greater China (incl. HK / Macau / Taiwan)

  • Hong Kong: Mainland arrivals up 13% on Day 1 of Golden Week (May 1). Hotel occupancy across Kowloon exceeded 90%. Miramar's Causeway Bay property reached 95% sold for the full May 1-5 window. Room rates rose 5-8% year-on-year. Spending remains cautious (day-trippers, short stays), with restaurant gains described as "limited" despite full hotels. (SCMP, May 4 2026; VisaHQ, May 3 2026)

  • China's Labour Day domestic travel is also at record levels, with hundreds of millions of trips within China and domestic hotel occupancy at record highs, keeping outbound share relatively modest. However, international bookings to Bangkok, Singapore, Seoul, Phuket and Bali remain strong. (Global Times, May 2026)

Policy and Borders

  • China Visa China's visa-free access expansion. China extended visa-free entry to citizens from Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Australia, New Zealand, Brunei and Malaysia in a 2026 rollout. This is generating strong bi-directional travel intent and contributing to the Labour Day demand spike into China-adjacent markets. (Best China Travel, Apr 2026)
  • Korea ETA South Korea ETA requirement effective January 1, 2026. Travellers from 67 countries and territories who previously entered visa-free must now apply for an online ETA before travel. South Korea extended the exemption window through December 31, 2026. This has added a booking-friction step that may modestly suppress spontaneous short-break travel. (VisasNews, 2026)
  • Japan eVisa Japan eVisa system expanded December 2025. As of December 15, 2025, Japan's eVisa is available to nationals of Australia, Brazil, Cambodia, Canada, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, Taiwan, United Kingdom and USA. This reduces friction for key source markets and supports continued inbound demand for Japan hotels. (MOFA Japan, Dec 2025)
  • US Restriction US pause on immigrant visas from 75 countries, including several APAC markets. Effective January 21, 2026, the US Department of State paused immigrant visa issuance to nationals of Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar and Thailand, among 75 total countries. This affects outbound travel to the US from these markets but has no direct inbound hotel impact in APAC. Worth monitoring for secondary effects on corporate travel flows. (Fortune, Jan 2026)
  • Hong Kong Crackdown Hong Kong tightening tourist scam enforcement during Golden Week. Authorities announced a crackdown on coerced shopping scams ahead of the Labour Day window. This is part of Hong Kong's broader effort to improve tourist experience quality ahead of its 50 million-visitor target for 2026. (SCMP, Apr 27 2026)
