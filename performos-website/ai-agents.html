Definition

What an AI agent actually is, in plain English

An AI agent is software that uses an AI model to decide what to do next, then does it. Unlike an automation that follows a fixed script, an agent picks its own steps within rules you set.

Agents matter for small business because they can handle work that varies, branches, and would normally need a person to chase it through. Done well, they recover hours of admin per week. Done badly, they create new problems faster than a tired human.

Use cases

Agents that pay off in small business

The most common agent use cases we see deliver real ROI for owner-led businesses.

Lead recovery

Missed-call response agent

Texts back missed calls within seconds, qualifies the lead, and books a callback or appointment. Stops you losing leads to whoever answered first.

Inbox

Inbound triage agent

Reads inbound email, classifies it, routes it, and drafts a response for review. Owners and ops stop being bottlenecks for every message.

Sales

Lead enrichment and follow-up

Researches new leads, drafts a personalised first touch, and tracks engagement. Sales reps spend more time talking, less time digging.

Knowledge

Internal answers agent

Answers staff questions about SOPs, policies, and product info using your own documents. Senior staff stop being a help desk.

Research

Weekly research and brief

Runs the same research every week and produces a clean brief in your inbox. Saves owners hours of repetitive scanning.

Ops

Document review agent

Reviews recurring documents (contracts, quotes, briefs) against a checklist, flags issues, and writes a summary for a human approver.

Where to use them

When an agent pays off, and when it is overkill

USE AN AGENT WHEN

Branching, judgement, and chasing

  • The task varies in input every time
  • A human has to chase it through multiple steps
  • There are clear rules you can enforce
  • The cost of a small mistake is low
  • The volume justifies the build
SKIP THE AGENT WHEN

Simple, fixed, or sensitive work

  • The steps are the same every time, use an automation
  • A single AI tool would solve it
  • Legal or financial review is required at every step
  • The task happens twice a month, not twice a day
  • You have not trained the team on AI fluency yet
